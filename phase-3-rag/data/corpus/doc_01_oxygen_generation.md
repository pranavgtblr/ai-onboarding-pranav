# Oxygen Generation & Solid Oxide Electrolysis (MOXIE-II)

## 1. System Overview
The Odyssey Mars Base relies on the MOXIE-II Solid Oxide Electrolysis system to extract breathable oxygen directly from the Martian atmospheric carbon dioxide (CO2). The Martian atmosphere consists of approximately 95.3% CO2, 2.6% nitrogen, and 1.9% argon. The system compresses Martian ambient air to 1.2 bar and heats it to an operating temperature of 800°C (1073 K) across yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) ceramic membranes.

## 2. Chemical Dissociation & Production Metrics
The fundamental chemical reduction reaction is:
2 CO2 -> 2 CO + O2
The primary electrolysis unit produces 2.4 kg of medical-grade oxygen per hour (99.6% purity). Each adult crew member consumes an average of 0.84 kg of O2 per terrestrial day. A base crew of 12 personnel requires 10.08 kg of oxygen daily, meaning the primary unit satisfies base breathing requirements in just 4.2 hours of continuous run-time daily. Excess oxygen is compressed into nickel-chromium high-pressure tanks at 350 bar.

## 3. Maintenance Protocols & Catalytic Degradation
The catalytic cathode is prone to carbon coking if the electric potential exceeds 2.1 Volts. Engineers must perform a reverse-sweep purging cycle every 250 operating hours using dry nitrogen. Filter assemblies (HEPA grade ceramic) must be replaced after every major dust storm or after 90 Martian sols. If membrane impedance climbs above 0.45 ohms, automatic shutdown is initiated.

## 4. Emergency Subsystems
In case of main electrolysis failure, the habitat maintains two redundant candle racks containing Sodium Chlorate (NaClO3) chemical oxygen candles. Each canister produces 600 liters of oxygen over a 45-minute exothermic burning period.
