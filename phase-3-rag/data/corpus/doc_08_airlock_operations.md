# Hyperbaric Airlock & Dust Mitigation Protocol

## 1. The Three-Chamber Airlock System
To prevent toxic Martian perchlorates and fine abrasive dust from contaminating the habitat atmosphere, Odyssey Base utilizes a three-chamber airlock system:
- Chamber 1 (Outer Vestibule): Dust knock-down and suit wash
- Chamber 2 (Suitlock): Depressurization / repressurization transfer chamber
- Chamber 3 (Inner Ready Room): Suit storage, umbilical charging, and pre-breathe

## 2. Electrostatic Shower & CO2 Dust Blowdown
Upon returning from an EVA, astronauts enter Chamber 1. An automated high-velocity carbon dioxide jet shower (25 m/s) strips surface dust from the suit fabric. Concurrently, an electrostatic ionizing grid neutralizes particle surface charges, causing dust to fall through a floor grating into an electrostatic collection hopper.

## 3. Pressure Transition Dynamics
Depressurization from habitat pressure (70.3 kPa / 10.2 psi) to Martian atmospheric pressure (0.75 kPa / 0.11 psi) takes 12 minutes. 92% of the evacuated air is recovered into vacuum recovery tanks rather than vented to Mars.

## 4. Perchlorate Contamination Thresholds
Martian regolith contains 0.5% to 1.0% toxic magnesium and calcium perchlorates. Habitat air is continuously monitored by ion chromatography sensors. Permissible exposure limit (PEL) in living quarters is strictly < 0.005 mg/m3.
