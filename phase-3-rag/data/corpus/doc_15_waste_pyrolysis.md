# Solid Waste Thermochemical Pyrolysis & Carbon Reclamation

## 1. Waste Stream Characterization
The 12-person habitat produces approximately 18 kg of dry solid waste daily, comprising food packaging, non-recyclable polymers, cotton and polyester clothing, fecal matter, and inedible crop biomass. Unprocessed waste represents a severe volume hazard and a loss of valuable hydrogen, carbon, and nitrogen atoms.

## 2. High-Temperature Vacuum Pyrolysis
Waste is shredded into 5 mm pellets and fed into an anaerobic rotary retort heated to 650°C at 0.1 bar. Without oxygen, the organic compounds do not burn; instead, they thermally decompose into three primary phases:
- Syngas (62% by volume): Carbon monoxide, methane, and hydrogen.
- Bio-oil Condensate (23%): Liquid hydrocarbons and water.
- Solid Biochar (15%): High-purity porous activated carbon.

## 3. Syngas Utilization
The syngas stream is scrubbed of hydrogen sulfide and fed directly into the Sabatier reactor or fuel cells, recovering 94% of the hydrogen content for water synthesis.

## 4. Biochar Application in Soil Remediation
The recovered activated biochar is rinsed with deionized water and used as a soil amendment in the greenhouse, binding trace heavy metals (cadmium, lead) and providing microscopic surface porosity for beneficial mycorrhizal fungi.
