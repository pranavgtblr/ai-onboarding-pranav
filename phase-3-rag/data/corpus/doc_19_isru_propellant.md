# Sabatier Catalytic Methanation & In-Situ Propellant Production

## 1. Chemical Reaction Fundamentals
Odyssey Base produces rocket propellant (liquid methane and oxygen) on Mars using the Sabatier methanation reaction:
CO2 + 4 H2 -> CH4 + 2 H2O (exothermic, delta H = -165 kJ/mol)
Martian atmospheric CO2 is combined with hydrogen brought from Earth or extracted from melted subsurface ice. The reaction takes place over ruthenium-on-alumina (Ru/Al2O3) catalyst pellets at 400°C and 4.0 bar pressure.

## 2. Water Electrolysis & Closing the Hydrogen Loop
The water produced by the Sabatier reaction is condensed and routed to high-temperature solid polymer electrolyte (SPE) electrolyzers:
2 H2O -> 2 H2 + O2
The oxygen is liquefied and stored in LOX tanks. The hydrogen is recycled back into the Sabatier reactor input stream, drastically reducing the net hydrogen feedstock needed from external sources.

## 3. Production Capacity & ERV Fueling
The ISRU plant operates continuously for 480 sols leading up to the arrival of the return crew. Total production capacity is 95 kg of LCH4 and 380 kg of LOX per sol. The entire 425-metric-ton propellant load for the Earth Return Vehicle is synthesized and verified 90 sols before crew departure from Earth.

## 4. Catalyst Poisoning Hazards
Martian atmosphere contains trace sulfur dioxide (SO2) and carbonyl sulfide (COS) at 0.1-0.5 ppm. These sulfur species irreversibly poison ruthenium catalysts. Atmospheric feed gas must pass through dual zinc oxide (ZnO) desulfurization guard beds operating at 220°C.
